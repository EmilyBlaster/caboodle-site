caboodle labs

where behavior design
meets ai experimentation.

Personal projects, prototypes, and proofs-of-concept. Each one testing whether AI actually changes how people work, or just ships features.

  1. 01

    Build the tool, not the feature. Labs exist to solve actual friction points that show up mid-project.

  2. 02

    Ground every prototype in learning science. Novelty is cheap. Behavioral evidence is the bar.

  3. 03

    Ship in public. Every experiment is a working URL you can visit, not a case study of what could be.

the bench.

Seven prototypes currently in rotation. Each has a live URL, a thesis, and a next-step roadmap. Click through to the working version.

    LAB
    001
    parallax + scrollytelling · interaction design live · experiment

    reading the rainforest.

    Three layers of rainforest, canopy to floor, stacked into one continuous scroll. You start in the bright crown and sink to the dark floor, and the whole scene runs on a single input: how far down the page you are. No timeline, no play button. Scroll it live, in place, or open it full screen.

    Thesis: Most digital learning treats scroll as a way to get from one box to the next. But scroll is motion, and motion carries feeling. Testing what that means for how people move through, and remember, an experience.

    002
    ai-built tool · workflow design live · 24-hour build

    inline stakeholder review system.

    A feedback workspace built directly into a client landing page using Claude Code. Pins anchored to exact positions, color-coded reviewers, a slide-out panel that groups comments by section with resolve tracking. No email threads. No annotated screenshots. No "jump on a quick call?"

    Thesis: Exploring whether AI-assisted code generation can solve real workflow problems mid-project, not just build features, but build tools, with a 24-hour turnaround and no design system to start from.

    003
    feedback design · adaptive ux live · prototype

    emotion-aware feedback system.

    Same scenario. Same learner response. Three completely different coaching outputs depending on emotional state. Submit once, see all three patterns. Submit three times and the system overrides your slider when it detects repeated struggle.

    Thesis: Grounded in Anthropic's April 2026 interpretability research on functional emotion in LLMs. Testing whether acknowledging emotional context before delivering feedback improves receptivity, and what that design pattern looks like in practice.

    006
    microlearning · behavioral nudges live · demo

    context-aware microlearning.

    Pick a moment. Giving difficult feedback. Onboarding someone new. Stuck on a design decision. Prepping for a stakeholder meeting. The phone comes alive with a personalized micro-intervention designed for that exact context.

    Thesis: The 2026 definition of microlearning is not shorter content. It is content that shows up at the moment of need, adapts to context, and triggers a specific decision or habit.

    LAB
    007
    applied llm + rag · self-directed live · solo build

    greenroom: a development copilot.

    An employee asks a real question in plain words. Greenroom answers from a curated people development library using real retrieval with citations, or an honest "not covered". It proposes a verb-first next step, then follows up to record whether the behavior actually changed. Built solo with Claude Code across six surfaces.

    Thesis: Most AI tools stop at delivering content. Greenroom is designed around the part they skip, behavior change, and proves it with a real eval harness: 22 of 22 cases pass, 100% refusal accuracy, and an independent judge model scoring groundedness at a mean of 92/100.

    • claude code
    • rag
    • voyage embeddings
    • agentic routing
    • eval harness

    LAB
    008
    bauhaus systems · homepage field test retired · preserved

    the poster: a homepage, field-tested.

    Seven interlocking Bauhaus planes, one per client engagement. Hover a plane and its textile pattern floods in; select it and the case study opens as a horizontal exhibit. Built entirely from clip-path polygons and CSS patterns, no libraries, and it recuts its own composition between landscape and portrait.

    Thesis: a consultancy homepage could be pure geometry. Field testing said no. Visitors evaluating a consultant need the service first and the art second, so the poster was measured, retired after two days in production, and filed here. Behavior change over checkbox training applies to my own website too.

    • clip-path polygons
    • css patterns
    • film-strip exhibits
    • orientation recut
    • zero libraries

01
the challenge

AI fluency at scale.
Without losing humans in the workflow.

AI enablement inside Fortune 500s tends to fail the same way: generic training modules stapled on top of existing workflows, no real measurement of adoption, no protection for the human side of the rollout. Airbnb's Employee Experience teams needed something different.

AI101: five performance-task modules. Ninety days kickoff to launch. Built to a Fortune 500 standard. Designed so the person at the desk on Monday morning would actually open the tool again on Tuesday.

02
the approach

Greenroom.
My methodology, in production.

I deployed Greenroom, the AI program leadership methodology I use with Fortune 500 clients. The framework starts with how the human actually works, maps where AI fits, and surfaces the adoption signals that matter more than completion certificates.

Behavior change is the foundation. AI fluency is the outcome. Both have to ship together or neither sticks.

Explore the Greenroom methodology in Labs →

03
the solution

Five labs, not lectures.
Performance task at every step.

Each of the five AI101 modules is a hands-on lab, not a lecture. Performance task at every step: learners build, critique, and ship real artifacts using AI inside their actual workflow. Each lab was designed backwards from a specific adoption moment, then stress-tested against the question that decides every AI rollout: would the person at the desk actually use this on Tuesday?

Action Mapping locked the design to those adoption moments. Kirkpatrick-aligned measurement framed the evaluation strategy. The Greenroom guardrails kept the program honest about what AI could and couldn't replace. The result: strategy clarity jumped 25 percentage points across the program, and every learner reported some or significant knowledge gain.

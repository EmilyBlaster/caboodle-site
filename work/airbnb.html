case study ENG-AIRBNB airbnb · 2026
airbnb

ai enablement
at fortune 500 scale.

Airbnb's Employee Experience teams ran a multi-track AI enablement effort built around how people actually adopt new tools. I designed and built five performance-task modules for AI101, the enablement program at the center of the initiative. The work shipped in ninety days and produced 100% reported knowledge gain across the program.

01
the challenge

AI fluency at scale.
Without losing humans in the workflow.

AI enablement inside Fortune 500s tends to fail the same way: generic training modules stapled on top of existing workflows, no real measurement of adoption, no protection for the human side of the rollout. Airbnb's Employee Experience teams needed something different.

AI101: five performance-task modules. Ninety days kickoff to launch. Built to a Fortune 500 standard. Designed so the person at the desk on Monday morning would actually open the tool again on Tuesday.

02
the approach

Performance task,
not lecture.

Each of the five modules I built for AI101 is a hands-on lab. Learners build, critique, and ship real artifacts using AI inside their actual workflow. The design starts with how the human already works, maps where AI fits, and surfaces the adoption signals that matter more than completion certificates.

Behavior change is the foundation. AI fluency is the outcome. Both have to ship together or neither sticks.

See my Greenroom example in Labs for the type of thinking and solution I bring to this kind of work →

03
the solution

Five labs.
Designed around real
adoption moments.

Each module starts with a real adoption moment Airbnb's Employee Experience teams face daily, then walks the learner through building the AI workflow that handles it. Each module ends with an artifact the learner can keep using on Monday morning.

Action Mapping locked the design to those adoption moments. Kirkpatrick-aligned measurement framed the evaluation. The result: strategy clarity jumped 25 percentage points across the program, and every learner reported some or significant knowledge gain.

inside a module · representative re-creation

step inside one performance task.

The five AI101 modules are internal to Airbnb. So here is the pattern instead of the content: one of the most common AI tasks on EX teams, turning messy meeting notes into a decision brief, rebuilt as a click-through so you can feel how a performance task works.

The pattern is real. The content is not Airbnb's. Every name, number, and note below is invented for public view.

step 1 · the adoption moment

monday, 9:14 am.

You just left a 45-minute stakeholder sync. Your director needs a decision brief by noon.

  • actions: A sends Beacon demo invite. M drafts success metrics.

In a lecture course, this is where a video about summarization plays. In a performance task, this is the assignment. Real input, real deadline. The module starts where the work starts.

01
Check the numbers
The draft calls Atlas "the clear cost leader." Your notes say the opposite: that price drew the hardest objection in the room. The model inverted a key fact. This is the most common synthesis failure there is.
02
Check the certainty
"Will finish within four weeks" upgrades your notes' 4 to 6 weeks, unconfirmed, into a promise your director will repeat upward. AI rounds uncertainty up to confidence. The human pass rounds it back down.
03
Check what's missing
The end-of-month deadline, the entire reason this brief exists, appears nowhere in the draft. The model summarized the meeting and buried the decision.

also shipped · a skill for people managers

a coach that meets managers where they are.

Beyond the five modules, I designed and built a Claude skill for people managers facing difficult conversations: performance concerns, role changes, hard feedback. It's grounded in the company's five decision-making best practices, and it doesn't live in a course. It lives wherever the manager already works.

The manager brings a real conversation they're dreading. The skill coaches the preparation: pressure-tests the framing against the decision practices, surfaces what's missing, rehearses the hard moments. Every manager gets a different experience because every manager brings a different situation. The tool adapts to the human, never the reverse.

primary surface

cowork

Built for Cowork, where managers already plan their week. The prep happens next to the work it's about, not in a separate tab they have to remember to open.

same skill

claude chat

Flexible enough to run in plain chat for the five-minutes- before-the-meeting rehearsal, when the manager needs a coach, not a course.

same skill

claude code

And it follows technical managers into Claude Code, the surface where they already spend the day. Zero new tools to adopt.

The skill and the five practices stay internal to Airbnb. The design pattern is the point: one skill, three surfaces, zero new tabs. Adoption by meeting people in their flow of work, not by asking them to come to the training.

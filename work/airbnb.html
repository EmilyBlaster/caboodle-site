Airbnb's Employee Experience teams ran a multi-track AI enablement effort built around how people actually adopt new tools. 01
the challenge

AI fluency at scale.
Without losing humans in the workflow.

AI enablement inside Fortune 500s tends to fail the same way: generic training modules stapled on top of existing workflows, no real measurement of adoption, no protection for the human side of the rollout. Airbnb's Employee Experience teams needed something different.

AI101: five performance-task modules. Ninety days kickoff to launch. Built to a Fortune 500 standard. Designed so the person at the desk on Monday morning would actually open the tool again on Tuesday.

02
the approach

Performance task,
not lecture.

Each of the five modules I built for AI101 is a hands-on lab. Learners build, critique, and ship real artifacts using AI inside their actual workflow. The design starts with how the human already works, maps where AI fits, and surfaces the adoption signals that matter more than completion certificates.

Behavior change is the foundation. AI fluency is the outcome. Both have to ship together or neither sticks.

See my Greenroom example in Labs for the type of thinking and solution I bring to this kind of work →

03
the solution

Five labs.
Designed around real
adoption moments.

Each module starts with a real adoption moment Airbnb's Employee Experience teams face daily, then walks the learner through building the AI workflow that handles it. Each module ends with an artifact the learner can keep using on Monday morning.

Action Mapping locked the design to those adoption moments. Kirkpatrick-aligned measurement framed the evaluation. The result: strategy clarity jumped 25 percentage points across the program, and every learner reported some or significant knowledge gain.
